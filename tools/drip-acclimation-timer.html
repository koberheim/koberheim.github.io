IVMethod & limits

Drip acclimation slowly mixes your tank's water into the bag or container over time, so temperature, pH, hardness and salinity shift gradually instead of all at once — the sudden version is what causes osmotic shock. The default guideline here is 30–45 minutes at 2–4 drops per second for typical community fish, stretched to 60–90 minutes at a slower rate for shrimp and a handful of sensitive species — pulled from the same water-sensitive/advanced tags used by the stocking calculator's species register, so the two tools agree with each other.

This is a guideline timer, not species-specific veterinary guidance, and it doesn't know your fish's actual condition. Watch the animal, not just the clock — if it looks stressed (gasping, erratic swimming, resting oddly) before the timer ends, that's more important than finishing the countdown. Livebearer fry, wild-caught fish, and anything arriving from a very different water source than yours generally benefit from the longer end of any range.

The tap-to-measure tool needs at least 4 taps to produce a rate; more taps (aim for 8–10) average out your timing better. It measures your tapping, as a stand-in for counting real drops — it doesn't see your actual drip line.

VCommon questions

Do I need to drip acclimate every fish?

For most hardy community fish arriving from a local shop with broadly similar water, a simpler float-and-mix acclimation is often enough. Drip acclimation earns its extra time for sensitive species, shrimp, wild-caught fish, anything that traveled a long distance or arrived stressed, and whenever your water parameters differ noticeably from where the fish came from.

What if I don't have airline tubing for a real drip line?

A slower, cruder version still helps: float the bag to match temperature, then add small splashes of tank water to the bag every few minutes instead of a continuous drip, roughly doubling the volume by the end of your target time. It's less precise than a siphon line, but far better than dumping fish straight in.

The timer says a different duration than I expected — why?

The default 30–45 minutes is a general-purpose starting point; selecting a species above adjusts it using that species' care flags (sensitivity, whether it's an invertebrate, etc.) in the same database the stocking calculator uses. You can always type your own number into the duration field — the recommendation is a starting point, not a rule.
