How this works (and what it isn't)

This calculator uses the surface-area rule — roughly 1″ of slim-bodied fish per 12 sq. in. of water surface for a standard, well-filtered tank — instead of the widely-repeated but inaccurate “1 inch per gallon” rule, which ignores oxygen exchange, filtration and tank shape entirely. On top of that base budget, each species carries a rough bioload weight so that heavier waste-producers (like plecos or goldfish) count for more than a same-length tetra.

This is a planning estimate, not a guarantee. It checks for a handful of well-known conflicts (fin-nippers with long-fin fish, goldfish mixed with tropical community fish, adult predators with very small fish, schooling fish kept in too-small groups) — it is not a full compatibility matrix. Always research the adult size, temperament and specific care needs of any species before you buy, cycle every new tank fully, and go slower than the calculator allows if you're new to the hobby. Species data here covers ~25 common beginner freshwater fish and invertebrates, not the full hobby — if you keep more exotic species or a saltwater tank, a larger tool may serve you better.

Common questions

Is the “1 inch of fish per gallon” rule wrong?

It's incomplete rather than flat-out wrong — it was never a bad starting point for small tetras in a small tank, but it ignores tank shape, filtration, and how heavily different species pollute the water. A tall, narrow 20 gallon holds noticeably fewer fish safely than a short, wide 20 gallon, and a single goldfish produces far more waste than an inch of neon tetra. See our surface-area rule guide for the longer explanation.

What counts as "overstocked"?

There's no single hard line — it's a spectrum of risk. Above roughly 100% of the budget this calculator estimates, ammonia and nitrite can build up faster than your filter and water changes can process it, especially in a newer, not-fully-cycled tank. Going a little over isn't automatically dangerous if you're doing frequent water changes and have strong filtration; going a lot over usually is.

Does a planted tank really let me keep more fish?

Somewhat, yes — live plants use ammonia/nitrate as a nutrient and can add some daytime oxygen, so a heavily (not lightly) planted tank gets a modest bump in this calculator. It's not a substitute for filtration or water changes, and a few plants scattered around a tank won't move the number much.

Why isn't my fish in the species list?

The list intentionally covers ~25 common beginner freshwater species, not the whole hobby — keeping it small is what keeps the data accurate and the page fast. If you keep more specialized species, a larger database like AqAdvisor will have more coverage; feel free to suggest an addition on GitHub.
